Thunderbird

Thunderbird is free, works on Windows, Mac and Linux, and handles mail, calendars, contacts and encrypted mail all in one app. It's the one I'd recommend if you don't already have a favorite. You can get it from thunderbird.net.

Before you start, make an app password for Thunderbird and keep that tab open.

Add your account

  1. Open Thunderbird. The first time, it goes straight to the account setup screen. If you already use Thunderbird for another account, open the menu (≡) and choose New Account → Email.
  2. Fill in:
    • Your full name: the name people see when you send them mail.
    • Email address: you@rootiest.org
    • Password: paste your app password.
  3. Leave Remember password ticked and click Continue.
  4. Thunderbird looks up the settings and shows them with IMAP selected and mx.rootiest.dev as the server. The provider shows as rootiest.org, which is expected. Click Done.

Your folders show up in the left pane and your mail starts downloading.

Calendars and address books

After the account is added, Thunderbird may offer to connect the calendars and address books it found on the server. If it does, go ahead. It uses the same app password.

If it doesn't offer, add them by hand:

Calendar

  1. Switch to the calendar (the calendar icon on the left) and click New Calendar.
  2. Choose On the Network and click Next.
  3. For Username enter your full address. For Location enter https://mx.rootiest.dev/dav/cal/. Click Find Calendars.
  4. Enter your app password when it asks, and tick Use Password Manager to remember this password.
  5. Pick the calendars you want and click Subscribe.

Address book

  1. Switch to the address book (the contacts icon on the left).
  2. Click the arrow next to New Address Book and choose Add CardDAV Address Book.
  3. For Username enter your full address. For Location enter https://mx.rootiest.dev/dav/card/. Click Continue.
  4. Enter your app password when it asks, then pick the address books to add.

Sending from an alias

If you have an alias, add it as an identity: open Account Settings, select your Rootiest account, click Manage Identities…, then Add… and enter the alias. After that you can pick it in the From box when you write a message.

Encrypted mail

Thunderbird has OpenPGP built in. See Encrypted mail for how to publish your key so others can find it.

If something goes wrong

  • "Thunderbird failed to find the settings": click Configure manually and use the manual settings.
  • "Login to server failed" or it keeps asking for a password: the app password is wrong or was deleted. See App passwords and make a new one.
