Thunderbird for Android is a free, open-source mail app from the people who make Thunderbird. Get it from the Play Store or F-Droid.

Before you start, make an app password for your phone. The easiest way is to do it on a computer and type it in on the phone, or make it in the phone's browser and copy it from there.

Add your account

  1. Open Thunderbird. The first time, tap Get started. If you already have an account in it, open the menu, go to Settings and tap Add account.
  2. Enter your address, you@rootiest.org, and tap Next.
  3. Thunderbird looks up the settings and shows the servers it found, with mx.rootiest.dev for both incoming and outgoing mail. Tap Next.
  4. Paste your app password and tap Next (or Sign in).
  5. Fill in your name as you want people to see it, and give the account a name if you like. Tap Next, then Done on the last screen.

The exact wording on these screens changes a bit between versions, but the steps are the same: address, check the settings, app password, your name.

If it can't find the settings

Choose to set the account up manually and use the manual settings: IMAP on mx.rootiest.dev port 993 with SSL/TLS, and SMTP on mx.rootiest.dev port 465 with SSL/TLS. The username is your full address both times.

Calendars and contacts

Thunderbird for Android only does mail. To sync your calendars and contacts with your phone's own Calendar and Contacts apps, use DAVx⁵. See Calendars and contacts.

Getting new mail quickly

If new mail shows up late, open the account's settings and make sure push is on for your inbox. Android's battery saving can also hold the app back, so you may need to allow Thunderbird to run in the background in your phone's battery settings.
