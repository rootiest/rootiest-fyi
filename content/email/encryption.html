OpenPGP lets people send you mail that only you can read. You have a key pair: a public key that anyone may have, and a private key that stays on your devices. Mail encrypted with your public key can only be opened with your private key.

The tricky part has always been getting your public key to the people who want to write to you. Rootiest Mail can publish it for you in a Web Key Directory (WKD). Once it's there, mail apps that support WKD (Proton, Thunderbird, GnuPG, Mailvelope and others) can look up your key from your address alone and encrypt to you without asking.

This is completely optional. Nothing is published unless you ask for it, and no key is ever made for you.

What you need

  • An OpenPGP key pair you made yourself. Thunderbird can make one for you (Tools → OpenPGP Key Manager → Generate → New Key Pair), or use GnuPG or any other OpenPGP app.
  • Your Rootiest address has to be one of the key's user IDs. It's fine if the key has other addresses on it too. Only the part for your Rootiest address gets published, so your other addresses stay private.

Publish your key

  1. Export your public key as text. In Thunderbird: Tools → OpenPGP Key Manager, select your key, then File → Export Public Key(s) To File. With GnuPG: gpg --armor --export you@rootiest.org.
  2. Email the exported file (the one that starts with -----BEGIN PGP PUBLIC KEY BLOCK-----) as an attachment to register-key@rootiest.org. Send it from the Rootiest address the key is for: that's how I know the key really belongs to you.
  3. You'll get a reply once it's live.

Only ever send your public key. If the text says PRIVATE KEY anywhere, stop: that's the part nobody else should have.

Check that it worked

With GnuPG installed, this looks your key up the same way other people's apps will:

gpg --auto-key-locate clear,nodefault,wkd --locate-keys you@rootiest.org

If it prints your key, you're done.

What publishing means

  • Anyone who knows your address can download your public key. That's the whole point, and it's safe: a public key can only be used to encrypt mail to you and check your signatures.
  • Apps that find it may start encrypting mail to you automatically. Make sure you can read encrypted mail wherever you read your mail. Webmail can't decrypt it, so you'll need Thunderbird or another OpenPGP app for those messages.

Replacing or removing your key

Ask on the support desk, the same way. New lookups get the change right away. Apps and servers that already fetched your old key keep their copy until they check again, each on its own schedule. So for a while some people may still encrypt to the old key.

If your private key was lost or stolen, revoke it in your OpenPGP app and send the revoked public key to be published first. That's how other people's apps learn to stop using it.

A setting to leave alone

Your account page at mail.rootiest.dev has an Encryption at Rest setting and a Public Keys section. Those are something else: they make the server encrypt every message it stores for you. With that on, webmail and any app without your private key can't read the messages stored that way. Please leave it off unless you've talked it over on the support desk first.
