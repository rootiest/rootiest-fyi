Plus addresses

You can add a + and any word you like after your name, and the mail still comes to you. If your address is sam@rootiest.org, then sam+shopping@rootiest.org, sam+bank@rootiest.org and sam+anything@rootiest.org all land in your inbox. You don't need to set anything up first. Just make one up when you need it.

This is handy for two things:

  • Sorting. Give each site or service its own tag, then make a filter that moves mail sent to that address into its own folder. For example, a filter on To contains sam+newsletters@ can keep newsletters out of your inbox.
  • Spotting leaks. If you sign up at a shop as sam+someshop@rootiest.org and spam starts showing up addressed to that, you know exactly who shared or lost your address.

A few websites wrongly refuse addresses with a + in them. If one won't take it, use your plain address there.

Replies you send still come from your normal address unless you change it, so the tag stays private to whoever you gave it to.

Masked addresses

A masked address is a random throwaway address that delivers to your mailbox, like 80j7sm2nyecsbsir.a4v3rm1nbehew47rg3h6etn1@rootiest.org. Nothing in it points back to you. That's the difference from a plus address: anyone can work out your real address from sam+shop@rootiest.org, while a masked one gives nothing away. They're best for sign-ups, newsletters and one-off forms where you only need to receive mail.

To make one in webmail:

  1. Open Settings → Password & sign-in and scroll down to Masked addresses.
  2. Pick the domain, and if you like, note the website and a description, so you remember later where you used it.
  3. Click Create masked address, then Copy to put it on your clipboard.

Before you use one, know what it can't do:

  • You can't send from it. Masked addresses only receive.
  • Replying shows your real address. If you answer mail that came in on a masked address, the reply comes from your normal one. When you'll need to write back, use a plus address instead.
  • You can have up to 5. Turned-off addresses may still count, so delete the ones you're done with.

When a masked address starts getting spam, open Edit and turn it off. New mail to it bounces as if the address never existed, so the sender can't tell it was ever yours. Delete it if you won't want it back.

Mail that arrives on a masked address shows it in the To line, so a filter on To can sort it like a plus address.

Aliases

An alias is a second address that delivers to your same mailbox, like a nickname. Unlike plus addresses, aliases are set up for you: if you'd like one, ask on the support desk and say which address you want. I approve each one by hand, so it may take a day or two.

Once you have an alias, mail sent to it shows up in your inbox, and you can send from it too. Add it as an extra identity in your app:

  • Webmail: Settings → Account → Edit addresses…
  • Thunderbird: Account Settings → Manage Identities…
  • Apple Mail: the account's Email settings
