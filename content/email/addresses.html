Plus addresses

You can add a + and any word you like after your name, and the mail still comes to you. If your address is sam@rootiest.org, then sam+shopping@rootiest.org, sam+bank@rootiest.org and sam+anything@rootiest.org all land in your inbox. You don't need to set anything up first. Just make one up when you need it.

This is handy for two things:

  • Sorting. Give each site or service its own tag, then make a filter that moves mail sent to that address into its own folder. For example, a filter on To contains sam+newsletters@ can keep newsletters out of your inbox.
  • Spotting leaks. If you sign up at a shop as sam+someshop@rootiest.org and spam starts showing up addressed to that, you know exactly who shared or lost your address.

A few websites wrongly refuse addresses with a + in them. If one won't take it, use your plain address there.

Replies you send still come from your normal address unless you change it, so the tag stays private to whoever you gave it to.

Aliases

An alias is a second address that delivers to your same mailbox, like a nickname. Unlike plus addresses, aliases are set up for you: if you'd like one, ask on the support desk and say which address you want. I approve each one by hand, so it may take a day or two.

Once you have an alias, mail sent to it shows up in your inbox, and you can send from it too. Add it as an extra identity in your app:

  • Webmail: Settings → Identities
  • Thunderbird: Account Settings → Manage Identities…
  • Apple Mail: the account's Email settings
